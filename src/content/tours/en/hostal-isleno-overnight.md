If a day tour leaves you wanting more time in San Blas but a private cabin isn't in the budget, this is the answer: a bed in a shared island dormitory, and the cheapest way to wake up with the Caribbean twenty steps from where you slept.

## What you're actually booking

A bed, not a room. You share the dormitory with the other travellers on the island that night, and the bathrooms are shared too. Island lodging in Guna Yala is rustic by design — thatch, sea breeze, no air conditioning, no grid electricity to speak of — and the dorm is the most honest version of that.

For solo travellers this is usually the point rather than the compromise. Dinner is eaten together, the group that snorkelled the same reef that afternoon ends up on the same beach after dark, and by breakfast you know everyone's name. If you'd rather not talk to anybody, book a cabin instead.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the dormitory mixed?

Yes — it's a shared island dorm, not gender-separated rooms. If that isn't for you, the private cabin options solve it without changing anything else about the stay.

### Are the bathrooms shared?

They are, and they're basic. This is off-grid island infrastructure, not a hostel in a city: expect cold water and simple facilities, and pack your own towel and soap because neither is provided.

### Can I charge my phone, and is there wifi?

There's no wifi on any island and the cell signal is unreliable at best. Power is limited and outlets are not something to count on, so bring a power bank if being reachable matters to you.

### Can I lock up my valuables?

Assume you can't, and travel accordingly — bring only what you'd be comfortable keeping with you. It's the same advice we'd give for any dorm anywhere, with the added detail that there is nowhere in Guna Yala to replace a lost passport.

### Can couples book the dormitory?

You can, and plenty do to save money, but you'll be sleeping in a shared room. Couples who want a night to themselves are usually happier in a private cabin.

### How does the second day work?

The same for every stay here: breakfast, then a boat tour to two more islands with a stop at the Natural Pool, back to the island to check out, and the return to Panama City around midday.
