Ask a San Blas guide which reef has the best snorkeling and the answer comes back the same: Cayos Holandeses. It is the farthest point most tours reach — a turquoise lagoon held inside a ring of coral, well out beyond the busier cays near the mainland.

## Why distance is the point

What protects this reef is exactly what makes it awkward to reach. Fewer boats make the longer crossing, so there is less traffic over the coral, and the difference is visible: the coral here is noticeably better preserved than on the reefs closer to Carti that receive boat after boat every operating day. You are not paying for a longer ride, you are paying for what the longer ride keeps away.

## What changes when you book this tour

Because of the extra distance, the Dutch Cays tour runs on its own rules, and all three are worth knowing before you commit:

- **Pickup around 4:30 AM**, earlier than the 5:00–5:45 AM standard for every other day tour, because the boat crossing is longer.
- **A minimum group size applies** per booking — the crossing is not run for one or two people.
- **No child discount.** On this tour adults and children pay the same rate, unlike the other San Blas day tours.

## What this stop is not

It is a reef and a lagoon, not an island with amenities. There is no infrastructure to speak of, no shade built for visitors, nothing to buy. It is also not a diving destination: standard San Blas tours do not include scuba with tanks, here or anywhere else in the archipelago. This reef is at its best a couple of meters below your mask.

## Choosing between this and the VIP tour

Be honest about your priority. If reef quality is the whole reason you are going to San Blas, this is the tour, and the 4:30 AM alarm is the price. If snorkeling is one good part of a day you mostly want to spend on beaches, the VIP day tour includes solid guided snorkeling closer to shore on a normal schedule and without a minimum group.

## Good to know

- **Best for:** snorkelers who care about coral condition rather than island scenery.
- **Facilities:** none — bring water, sun protection and anything else you will want during the day.
- **Sea conditions:** a longer open-water crossing means the day is more exposed to weather than a short hop from Carti; if conditions are unsafe the operator reschedules or refunds in full.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does the Dutch Cays tour leave so early?
Pickup is around 4:30 AM because the boat crossing from Carti out to Cayos Holandeses is considerably longer than to the islands nearer the mainland. Starting earlier is what makes it possible to reach the reef, spend real time in the water, and still be back in Panama City the same night.

### Is there a minimum number of travelers for this tour?
Yes. Because of the extra distance, the Dutch Cays tour requires a minimum group size per booking. If your party is smaller than that minimum, the tour can still run when other travelers book the same date, so it is worth asking about specific dates rather than assuming.

### Do children pay less on the Dutch Cays tour?
No. This is the one San Blas day tour where adults and children pay the same rate — there is no child discount, precisely because the cost of the tour is driven by the crossing rather than by the number of seats.

### Is Cayos Holandeses worth it compared with the VIP day tour?
It depends on what you came for. Cayos Holandeses has the best-preserved coral in the archipelago, and nothing closer to shore matches it. The VIP tour has a friendlier schedule, no minimum group, a child rate and a shipwreck snorkel. Neither is better in the abstract; they are built for different priorities.

### Can I stay overnight at Cayos Holandeses?
No. These are uninhabited cays and a reef, visited as a day stop. Overnight stays in San Blas happen on inhabited islands with Guna-run cabins, and they are booked by cabin type rather than by a named island.
