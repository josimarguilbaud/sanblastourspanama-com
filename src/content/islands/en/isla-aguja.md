Isla Aguja is chosen for a reason that has nothing to do with what is on the island. It is one of the closest cays to the Carti embarkation point, which means the shortest stretch of open water between the boat and a beach — and for a lot of travellers, that is the single most welcome thing about it.

## The shortest crossing of the day

Getting to San Blas is already a long morning: a 5:00–5:45 AM pickup in Panama City, then roughly two and a half hours by 4x4 on a winding mountain road, then a speedboat. By the time you reach Carti, a short hop to the first beach is worth more than a spectacular one an hour further out. Isla Aguja is what a tour puts at the front of the day for exactly that reason.

## Why it works with small children

The water around Isla Aguja is notably calm and shallow close to shore, without the stronger currents you meet further out in the archipelago. Add fine white sand and a short boat ride and you have the easiest possible introduction for young kids, or for anyone who would rather not begin the day in deep open water. Life jackets are mandatory in the boats for children and adults alike.

## Where it sits in the itinerary

Because of that proximity, Isla Aguja tends to be the first or the last stop of a multi-island day. It appears on the Classic day tour, the simplest of the itineraries — door-to-door transport, one island, the Natural Pool and lunch on the sand, with no guided reef snorkeling and no community visit built in.

## Nobody lives here

Isla Aguja is uninhabited: there are basic shade shelters and no permanent settlement, no shop and no drinking water. That is the norm rather than the exception among the smaller cays, and it means the day works on what the boat brings — lunch, water and whatever you packed yourself.

## Who San Blas is not built for

Worth saying plainly, because a calm beach does not make the journey gentle. San Blas is not recommended for pregnant travellers, for people with back or hip conditions, or for anyone with a serious medical condition, and it is not wheelchair accessible. The 4x4 road and the boat transfers are the reason, not the islands.

## Good to know

- **Best for:** families with young children, short transfers, an easy start before more adventurous stops.
- **Facilities:** basic shade shelters only, and no settlement of any kind on the island.
- **Typical itinerary role:** first or last stop on a multi-island day tour, given how close it lies to the mainland embarkation point.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Isla Aguja really one of the closest islands to Carti?
Yes — it is among the nearest cays to the embarkation point, which is precisely why tours use it. The practical benefit is less time crossing open water and more time on the sand, which matters most at the start and end of an already long day.

### Is Isla Aguja safe for small children?
It is one of the gentler options for them. The water close to shore is shallow and calm, without the stronger currents found deeper into the archipelago, and the short crossing means less time in the boat. Children wear life jackets on the water like everyone else.

### Do young children pay for a San Blas day tour?
Children aged 0 to 4 typically travel free on our day tours, and older children usually have their own rate. The one exception in the catalogue is the Cayos Holandeses tour, where adults and children pay the same. Confirm the current child rate when you book your dates.

### I get seasick — what should I know?
The leg to prepare for is not the boat but the road: the 4x4 route to Carti is a winding mountain road, and travellers prone to motion sickness usually take something before departure rather than during. The crossing to Isla Aguja itself is one of the shortest of the day.

### Is there shade or anything else on Isla Aguja?
There are basic shade shelters and nothing more — no shop, no drinking water, no permanent settlement. Bring biodegradable sunscreen, a hat and enough water for the stop, and count on the boat rather than the island for everything else.
