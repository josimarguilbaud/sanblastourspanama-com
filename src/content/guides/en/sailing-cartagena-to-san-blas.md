Search "Cartagena to San Blas" and you will find multi-day sailing trips advertised with photographs of exactly the water you were hoping for. They are real, and people enjoy them. But it is worth being clear about what that trip is, because it is not primarily a San Blas tour — it is a border crossing that passes through San Blas.

## What the route actually is

Sailboats and catamarans run multi-day crossings between Cartagena, in Colombia, and Panama, typically taking **four to five days**. The route sails through part of the San Blas archipelago before continuing to the Panamanian mainland, usually landing near Carti or Portobelo. These trips are operated by independent sailing companies and charter owners — **we do not run them** — and they exist because there is no road between Colombia and Panama, which makes a boat one of the few ways to make that crossing overland-adjacent.

## What the days look like

The bulk of the itinerary is time at sea. Some of it is open water between the two countries; a portion of it is inside the archipelago, where boats anchor and passengers swim. You will see San Blas, but you will see the part that lies on the sailing line rather than a route designed around the best cays, reefs and community stops, because the boat's job is to get from one country to the other.

## The honest trade-offs

- **It is a multi-day commitment**, not a visit you can slot into a Panama City itinerary.
- **Comfort varies enormously by vessel.** Cabin quality, food, crew and the amount of open-water motion differ from one boat to the next far more than they do between tour operators on land.
- **Seasickness is a genuine factor** on a crossing of several days, in a way it simply is not on a short transfer from Carti.
- **You are prepaying a stranger.** Since these are independent operators, read recent reviews carefully and understand the cancellation terms before transferring money.

## If sailing itself is what appeals

If the attraction is the boat rather than the border, the archipelago has its own answer: private, fully crewed [yacht and catamaran charters](/yachts) that sail San Blas on an itinerary you set, with no obligation to end up in another country. That is a different product from a Colombia–Panama passage and usually a better fit for travelers who want to sail *San Blas* rather than sail *through* it.

## If San Blas itself is the goal

If what you actually want is time in the archipelago — several islands, snorkelling over the shipwreck reef, a Guna community visit, perhaps a night in a beachfront cabin — a day tour or overnight stay leaving from **Panama City** gets you there in hours rather than days, on a fixed schedule, coordinated directly with a real person rather than wired to an independent boat.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does the sailing trip from Cartagena to San Blas take?

Typically four to five days for the full crossing between Cartagena and Panama, including the stretch through the San Blas archipelago. It is a multi-day passage rather than an excursion.

### Is it a San Blas tour or a Colombia–Panama border crossing?

Fundamentally a crossing. San Blas is the beautiful middle section of a journey whose purpose is to move you between two countries, so the stops are chosen by the sailing route rather than by which cays are best.

### Do you operate these sailing trips?

No. They are run by independent sailing companies and charter owners. Everything sold on this site departs from Panama City by 4x4 and boat, or is a private charter within the archipelago.

### Will I see much of San Blas on a sailing crossing?

A portion of it. You will spend time in the archipelago and swim from the boat, but you will not cover the range of islands, reefs and community stops that a dedicated San Blas itinerary is built around.

### Is the crossing rough?

It can be. Several days in open water is a different experience from the short crossing between Carti and the islands, and motion sensitivity is worth taking seriously before committing to a multi-day passage.

### What should I check before booking with a sailing company?

Recent reviews from the last season, what the cabins and meals actually are, how many passengers share the boat, what happens if weather delays departure, and the refund terms. Ask all of it in writing before paying a deposit.
