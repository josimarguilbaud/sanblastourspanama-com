Every island in San Blas has a beach, so "which is best" is the wrong question until you say what you want from it. A cay that is perfect for a photograph can be a poor place to swim with a four-year-old, and the one with the best reef has almost no sand at all. Here is the shortlist, sorted by what each one actually does well.

## Best for snorkelling: Perro Chico

Small enough to walk around in ten minutes, and famous for what lies just offshore: a sunken hull in shallow water, now an artificial reef busy with fish. [Perro Chico](/islands/perro-chico) is the most requested stop on San Blas itineraries because the wreck is accessible to complete beginners while still being worth the swim for experienced snorkellers. The beach itself is a narrow strip — somewhere to dry off between sessions rather than a place to spend the day.

## Best postcard beach: Chichime

Two cays side by side, separated by a channel shallow enough to wade at low tide. [Chichime](/islands/chichime) is the most photographed sand in the archipelago for good reason: powder-white, lined with leaning palms, and fronted by water that shifts from pale turquoise to deep blue within a few steps. A small Guna community lives on one of the two cays, so ulus pulled up on the sand and nets drying in the sun are part of the picture.

## Best for families: Isla Aguja

Close to the Carti embarkation point, which means less time crossing open water with restless children, and ringed by calm, shallow water without the stronger currents further out. [Isla Aguja](/islands/isla-aguja) is often the first stop of the day for exactly that reason.

## Best for island-hopping: Limones Cays

Not one beach but a cluster of them, close enough together that you can swim off one cay, re-board and be swimming off the next five minutes later. The [Limones Cays](/islands/cayos-limones) also hold some of the clearest water in the archipelago, which is saying something here.

## Best for space: Perro Grande

Most San Blas cays are tiny. [Perro Grande](/islands/perro-grande) is the genuine exception, with wide stretches of white sand that do not feel shared with everyone on the boat, and calm turquoise water better suited to floating than to chasing fish. Simple beachfront cabins make it an option for an overnight stay too.

## Best quiet, premium stop: Isla Pelícano

Set further out toward the Dutch Cays, [Isla Pelícano](/islands/isla-pelicano) sees fewer boats and clearer water than the cays near the mainland, and it carries a small piece of pop-culture fame from its appearance in Netflix's *Money Heist*. The extra boat time means it usually turns up on VIP or private itineraries.

## The one that is not a beach at all

The Natural Pool is on almost every itinerary and it has no shore: it is a sandbar in open water where you wade in from the boat, waist-deep, with the sea in every direction and starfish visible on the pale bottom. It is worth knowing in advance that this stop is about standing in water, not lying on sand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is the most beautiful beach in San Blas?

Chichime takes the most photographs — two palm-lined cays, white sand and water that changes colour within a few metres. If you define beauty by clarity of water rather than by the shoreline, the outer cays such as Limones and Isla Pelícano are the stronger answer.

### Can I visit these beaches without a tour?

Not practically. None of them are reachable independently: every one is accessed by boat from the Carti embarkation point as part of an organised day tour or overnight stay, and comarca permits and boat hire would otherwise be your problem to solve.

### Which beach is best with small children?

Isla Aguja, for calm shallow water and a short boat transfer, and the Natural Pool, where the water is waist-deep and children can stand the whole time. Isla Diablo is another easy option, with sheltered swimming and shade.

### Which San Blas island appeared in Money Heist?

Isla Pelícano, known by its Guna name Isla Gorgidub, was used as a filming location for the Netflix series. It sits further out toward the Dutch Cays, so reaching it usually means a VIP or private itinerary.

### Are San Blas beaches crowded?

By Caribbean standards, no. Busy here means several boats sharing a cay in high season, not rows of loungers — there are no resorts, no beach clubs and no vendors beyond Guna artisans selling molas.

### Can I choose which beaches my tour visits?

On a shared tour, the route is set by the itinerary, the day's conditions and the weather, so specific cays are never guaranteed. On a fully private tour you help decide the day's islands within what is reasonable for timing and sea state.
