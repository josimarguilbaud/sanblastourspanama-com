San Blas is built for snorkelling and not for scuba diving, and knowing that before you book saves you from planning a trip around the wrong activity. The reefs here are shallow, warm and clear — which is exactly what makes them rewarding at the surface and unremarkable with a tank.

## The three signature spots

- **The Perro Chico shipwreck.** A sunken vessel lying in water shallow enough for complete beginners, now an artificial reef thick with coral growth and reef fish. It is the single most requested stop in the archipelago, and usually a short swim from the boat or the beach.
- **The Natural Pool.** Less a snorkel site than a wade: a sandbar where the water sits around waist height and the pale bottom makes starfish easy to spot. It suits nervous swimmers and children better than any reef does.
- **Cayos Holandeses (the Dutch Cays).** The best-preserved reef in San Blas, protected by nothing more sophisticated than distance — fewer boats make the longer crossing. It is included on select tours, which depart earlier to allow for the run out.

## Gear: what to expect and what to bring

Snorkel gear — mask, snorkel, fins — is **not always included as standard**, so check what your specific tour provides. Shared equipment is often available, but fit is the thing that ruins a snorkel and the thing you cannot fix on a boat: a leaking mask or fins a size out will cost you the stop. Anyone who snorkels regularly should bring their own mask at minimum.

A rash guard is worth more here than most people expect, less for warmth than for the hours of overhead sun you accumulate face-down at the surface.

## Can you scuba dive in San Blas?

The honest answer is that **standard San Blas day tours and overnight stays do not include scuba diving with tanks**. The appeal of this archipelago is clear, shallow water — reefs often only a few metres deep, best experienced floating over them. If certified tank diving is the centre of your holiday, San Blas is not the destination built around it, and we would rather say so now than sell you a day that disappoints.

## Choosing a tour if the reef is the point

If snorkelling is a pleasant part of a wider day, the VIP itinerary includes guided reef snorkelling at Perro Chico alongside the Natural Pool and a Guna community stop. If reef quality is the *reason* for the trip, the Dutch Cays tour is the one — it costs you an earlier alarm and carries a minimum group size, and it puts you on the least-trafficked coral in Guna Yala. ## Practical tips for the water

- **Reef-safe sunscreen only.** Ordinary sunscreen chemistry damages coral.
- **Life jackets are mandatory on the boats**, for adults and children, confident swimmers included.
- **Reapply and cover up.** Long sessions face-down burn shoulders and the back of the legs faster than anything on land.
- **Do not stand on the reef.** Adjust your mask in open water or at the boat.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to be a strong swimmer to snorkel in San Blas?

Not for most stops. The wreck at Perro Chico sits in shallow water close to the boat, and the Natural Pool is waist-deep wading rather than swimming. Tell your guide if you are not a confident swimmer — they place people accordingly, and life jackets are available throughout.

### Is snorkel gear included in the tour?

Not always. Some tours provide shared masks, snorkels and fins and some do not, so confirm for the specific tour you book. If fit matters to you, bring your own — it is the one piece of equipment nobody can substitute on the day.

### Can you scuba dive in San Blas?

Not as part of standard day tours or overnight stays. There is no tank diving built into these itineraries; the archipelago's reefs are shallow and are designed, in effect, to be enjoyed from the surface.

### Where is the San Blas shipwreck?

Off Perro Chico, in the Isla Perro group. The hull rests in shallow water just offshore and has become an artificial reef, which is why it is the most requested snorkelling stop in the archipelago.

### Which tour has the best snorkelling?

The Dutch Cays tour, without much argument — it reaches the most remote and best-preserved reef in San Blas. It requires an earlier departure and a minimum group size. The VIP day tour is the strongest option for guided snorkelling closer to shore.

### Do children snorkel too?

Yes, at the easier stops. The Natural Pool in particular works for young children because it is shallow enough to stand in, and life jackets are compulsory for children as for adults on every boat.

### Is visibility usually good?

At the wreck and on the outer reefs, generally yes — clear, shallow water is the archipelago's defining feature. Conditions still vary with weather and with how much boat traffic has passed through a spot that morning.
