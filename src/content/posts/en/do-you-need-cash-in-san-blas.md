**Yes. Guna Yala has no ATMs and the islands have no card terminals, so everything you spend inside the comarca is paid in US dollars, in cash. The one fixed cost is the entry tax — $22 for foreigners, $7 for Panamanian residents — which is not in any tour price. How much more you need depends on whether you come for a day, a few nights or a sailing trip.**

## Why there are no ATMs or card machines

Guna Yala is a self-governed indigenous territory of small island communities, and there are no bank branches or cash machines on the islands. Even where a business might want a card reader, mobile signal is patchy and most islands have little or no mains electricity, so a terminal that needs a connection would fail half the time. The economy you step into runs on cash, as it has for generations: molas, coconuts, fish and lobster change hands for dollars.

Panama's currency is the balboa, but the banknotes in circulation are US dollars, so there is nothing to exchange if you arrive with dollars. If you arrive with another currency, change it or withdraw in Panama City; nobody on an island will take euros or pesos.

## What you pay in cash, by type of trip

The tour price covers the core of each trip. Cash covers what sits around it.

| Trip | What the tour price covers | What you pay in cash |
|---|---|---|
| Day tour from Panama City | 4x4 transport, boat, lunch with a drink, Guna guide | Entry tax, extra drinks and snacks, tips, molas and crafts |
| Island stay (1–3 nights) | Transport, boat, cabin, three meals a day, a tour to two more islands | Entry tax, all drinks and snacks, alcohol, tips, crafts, optional extras such as a later check-out |
| Sailing trip or yacht charter | Depends on the boat and the contract | Anything bought from Guna vendors who come alongside, community fees on some islands, crafts, tips |

Two notes on the table. On day tours that include snorkelling, the gear is provided; on island stays it is not, so bring your own rather than counting on renting it. And the balance of a tour booked with a deposit is settled with the driver on the day, which is a separate question from your spending money; our [prices page](/prices) explains how paying works.

## The entry tax: the one payment you cannot skip

Every visitor pays the comarca's entry tax at the checkpoint on the road in: **$22 for foreigners and $7 for Panamanian residents** who show a passport or residency card. Children under 10 are exempt. It is charged per person, not per group, and it is separate from whatever you paid for the tour.

On our tours the driver can also take it by card, but treat that as a backup and carry it in cash, counted out per person in small bills, so the stop at the checkpoint takes a minute. The tax goes to the Guna authorities, not to the operator; [where the community tax goes](/blog/where-does-community-tax-go) explains who collects it and what it funds.

## Day tour: what the cash is actually for

On a day trip, your spending is short and predictable: the tax, drinks beyond the one served with lunch, tips for the captain and guide if you want to leave them, and crafts. Molas are the purchase people most often regret missing. They are sewn by Guna women and sold by the women who make them, and the price reflects the layers and hours of work; [molas explained](/blog/molas-explained) tells you what to look for before you buy.

## Island stays: the bar has no card machine either

Staying overnight changes the arithmetic. Meals are included, but drinks are not, and two or three evenings of soft drinks, beer and snacks add up faster than people expect. Optional extras, such as a later check-out on your last day, are arranged on the spot. Our [overnight stays guide](/guides/overnight-stays) lists exactly what each stay includes and leaves out. Lobster is a seasonal treat rather than a guarantee: from March through June, the Guna closed season and Panama's national one keep it off the menu.

## Sailing trips: no withdrawals for days

If you are crossing between Colombia and Panama by sailboat, or chartering a yacht for a few days among the cays, you can go several days without passing a single cash machine. Guna fishermen often come alongside anchored boats in their canoes offering fish, crab or lobster, and women sometimes come by with molas; both are cash sales. Sailboats also pay their own cruising fee to the Guna General Congress, a charge documented by anthropologist Mònica Martínez Mauri in [her study of Guna entry fees](https://journals.openedition.org/norois/6609), and some communities charge visitors a small fee to land on their island. Ask your captain which of these the fare already covers. If you are coming from Cartagena, bring enough dollars from Colombia; the [sailing route guide](/guides/sailing-cartagena-to-san-blas) covers the rest of the logistics.

## How much to bring

There is no single right figure, because the variable is what you buy, not what you need. A workable way to plan:

- **The entry tax**, per person, in exact small bills.
- **A daily allowance** for drinks, snacks and tips. On a day trip, roughly $30 to $50 per person beyond the tax covers most people; on a stay, count it per day.
- **A separate amount for crafts**, decided in advance, if you want a mola. It is the purchase most likely to exceed a casual budget.

## Where to get cash before you go

Withdraw in Panama City, ideally the day before you travel: pickups start around 5:00 AM and there is no stop for an ATM on the way. Panamanian bank machines usually charge a fee per withdrawal and cap the amount, so take out what you need in one or two goes rather than in small amounts. Ask for small notes ($1, $5, $10 and $20) or break larger ones at a supermarket; a $50 or $100 bill is hard to change on an island. Keep the cash in a dry bag, and split it between two places so one dropped pouch does not end your spending for the day.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are there ATMs in San Blas?

No. There are no ATMs anywhere in Guna Yala, on the islands or along the road from Panama City. Withdraw everything you need in the city before your trip.

### Can I pay by card on the San Blas islands?

Not on the islands. Drinks, snacks, crafts and tips are cash only. On our tours, the entry tax can also be paid by card to the driver at the checkpoint, but carrying it in cash is the safer plan.

### What currency is used in San Blas?

US dollars. Panama's official currency, the balboa, is pegged to the dollar and its banknotes are US dollar bills. Bring small notes; other currencies are not accepted on the islands.

### How much cash do I need for a San Blas day trip?

The entry tax ($22 for foreigners, $7 for Panamanian residents, children under 10 exempt) plus roughly $30 to $50 per person for drinks and tips, and more if you plan to buy a mola.

### Do sailboats pay fees in Guna Yala?

Yes. Sailboats pay a cruising fee to the Guna General Congress, and some communities charge a small landing fee. Whether your fare includes them depends on the boat, so ask your captain before you leave.
