**Choose a day tour if you have a single free day in Panama, want your own bed and air conditioning, or travel with young children. Choose an overnight stay if San Blas is the point of your trip: you get the islands after the day boats leave, sunset and sunrise and a slower pace, in exchange for rustic cabins and at least two days of your itinerary.**

We sell both formats, so this is not a pitch for one over the other. Both are good; they simply answer different questions.

## The two formats side by side

|---|---|---|

## What a day tour does well

A day tour is the most efficient way to see San Blas. One booking covers the 4x4, the boat, lunch and a Guna guide, you carry only a day bag, and you sleep in your own bed. It fits a short stay in Panama, a family with small children, or anyone who wants to see the archipelago before deciding whether to come back for longer.

The tours are not all the same, which is worth knowing before you compare them with a stay. The [Classic Day Tour](/tours/classic-day-tour) is the simplest: one island plus the Piscina Natural, no packed schedule. The [VIP Day Tour](/tours/vip-day-tour) adds guided snorkelling over the shipwreck near Isla Perro Chico and a stop in a Guna community. Others string several cays into one day.

The honest limits are about time. Around five hours of the day are spent getting there and back, so you have roughly six and a half hours on the water and sand. You arrive with the other boats from Cartí and leave with them in mid-afternoon, just as the light softens. And the day starts before dawn: a pickup at 5:00 AM is part of the deal.

## What staying overnight changes

The difference is not "the same day plus a bed". It is mostly about what happens after 3:00 PM.

- **The island empties out.** Once the day boats head back to Cartí, the cay belongs to its Guna hosts and a handful of guests. Most people who stay name that afternoon as the moment the trip changed.
- **Sunset, a dark sky and sunrise.** Guna Yala has almost no light pollution, so on a clear night the stars reach the horizon. The morning swim, before any boat arrives, is the calmest water you will see.
- **Better timing for the classic stops.** On a two-day stay, the island tour on the second morning reaches the Cayos Limones, the shipwreck snorkel and the Piscina Natural early, before the day-trip crowd.
- **Time with your hosts.** Three meals a day are cooked on the island, usually fish, rice, plantain and coconut, and the evenings are unhurried. Our guide to what you eat in San Blas explains the food.

## What you give up by staying

An overnight stay is rustic by design, and it is fair to say so plainly. Cabins have thatched roofs and simple beds; bathrooms are often shared; there is no air conditioning, no hot water and, on most islands, little or no electricity. There is no wifi, and mobile signal comes and goes. Mosquitoes appear at dusk, so repellent belongs in the bag; our [safety guide](/blog/is-san-blas-safe) covers the health side.

There are practical conditions too. Stays are booked by **cabin type**, not by named island, and the island is confirmed closer to your date according to availability. They need at least 60 days' notice, they run for one to three nights, and drinks, snorkel gear, towels and the comarca tax are not included.

## The five cabin types in one paragraph each

- **Hostal Isleño:** a shared dormitory, the most affordable way to wake up on an island, popular with solo travelers.
- **Cabaña Privada Clásica:** a simple private cabin on the beach for couples or small groups.
- **Cabaña Privada Deluxe:** the same privacy with a step up in comfort and finish.
- **Camping Bajo las Estrellas:** a tent under an open sky, for people who want the night itself to be the experience.
- **Cabaña Overwater:** a private cabin built over the water, the premium option and the one with the least availability.

## Can you combine them?

Yes, and plenty of people do it in stages: a day tour early in the trip to see whether the place suits them, then a night or two later on. If you only have two days, a one-night stay with late check-out, which keeps you on the island until about 3:00 PM, gives you a full afternoon, an evening, a morning and most of a second day for one round trip on the road.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is one night in San Blas enough?

For most people, yes. A one-night stay gives you the empty afternoon, sunset, sunrise and a morning island tour. Two nights add an unstructured day in the middle, which is when the place really slows down.

### Do overnight stays include transport from Panama City?

Yes. Every stay includes the round-trip 4x4 from your Panama City accommodation, the boat transfers, your cabin, three meals a day, a tour to two more islands, the Piscina Natural and a Guna guide.

### Is a day tour or a stay better with children?

A day tour is usually easier with young children, since they sleep in a familiar bed and avoid rustic bathrooms. Children under 4 stay free on overnight stays, so families with older children often enjoy a night on the island.

### Can I choose which island I sleep on?

Not exactly. Stays are booked by cabin type, and the island is confirmed nearer the date based on availability. If something specific matters, such as a dietary need, mention it when you book.

### Is it worth staying overnight without electricity or wifi?

That is precisely what most guests come for. A power bank covers your phone, and the lack of signal is what makes the evening feel like being somewhere else entirely.
