**Choose by goal, not by photo. A four-to-five-day sailing crossing from Cartagena is a border passage that spends part of its time in San Blas. A tour or cabin stay from Panama City is the most direct way to see the islands. A private crewed charter is San Blas on your own schedule, for travellers paying for privacy.**

## Option 1: the Cartagena–Panama sailing crossing

There is no road between Colombia and Panama. The roadless stretch is the Darién Gap, and it is often confused with San Blas; our post on [whether San Blas is part of the Darién Gap](/blog/is-san-blas-part-of-darien-gap) untangles the two. Because of that gap, sailboats and catamarans make a living carrying travellers between the countries, and their usual route runs through Guna Yala before landing on the Panamanian coast.

The honest description is a sea passage with a San Blas section in the middle. Days are split between open water and anchorages inside the archipelago where passengers swim and snorkel. You see the islands that lie on the sailing line, not a route designed around the best reefs or a community visit. Our [guide to the Cartagena route](/guides/sailing-cartagena-to-san-blas) covers the details; the points that decide it for most people are these:

- **We do not operate these crossings.** You are dealing with independent companies and boat owners, so read reviews from the last season and get the cancellation terms in writing before paying.
- **Comfort varies by boat far more than by brand.** Cabin size, food, crew and the number of passengers differ enormously.
- **Seasickness is real.** Several days in the open Caribbean are a different proposition from a short crossing between Cartí and the islands.
- **It involves an international border.** Exit and entry formalities are part of the trip; ask the operator exactly how they are handled on their route.

If you are travelling between the two countries anyway, the crossing turns a necessary journey into a memorable one. If you are not, it is a long way to go for a partial view of San Blas.

## Option 2: a tour or cabin stay from Panama City

This is how most visitors reach the archipelago, because it is built around the islands rather than a destination beyond them. A 4x4 picks you up in Panama City around 5:00 AM, the drive to the port of Cartí takes about two and a half hours, and a boat does the rest; our guide to [getting to San Blas](/guides/how-to-get-to-san-blas) explains each leg.

Within that format there are two decisions. The first is one day or longer: a day tour returns you to your hotel in the evening, while a cabin stay of one to three nights gives you sunrise, sunset and the islands without the day-trippers. The second is which itinerary: some are built around the Perro Chico wreck, some around a community visit, one around the remote reef at Cayos Holandeses.

The trade-offs are the reverse of the sailing crossing. Schedules are fixed, boats are shared unless you book a private tour, and the cabins are deliberately simple: thatched roofs, shared bathrooms in some types, no air conditioning. In exchange, the money goes to Guna-run tourism, the logistics are a single booking, and every hour in the archipelago is spent where the itinerary means to be.

## Option 3: a private crewed charter

A [private yacht or catamaran charter](/yachts) is the opposite of a shared boat. A captain and crew from our sailing partners handle navigation and meals, and the itinerary is built with you: a sunrise anchorage, the outer reefs a standard day tour never reaches, a night anchored off a quiet cay.

It is not a border crossing, and it is not exempt from the territory's rules. A charter still sails inside Guna Yala, so community permissions apply, scuba diving is still off the table, and the comarca's fees still exist; ask the charter how they are handled. Charters are quoted per boat, which makes them more reasonable for a group sharing the cost than for a couple, and they need planning ahead.

If what you want is privacy for a single day rather than a week afloat, there is a middle ground: the [Super VIP private day tour](/tours/super-vip-private-day-tour) gives your group its own 4x4 and its own boat for the day.

## Who each option is wrong for

- **The sailing crossing** is wrong for anyone with a fixed number of days in Panama, anyone prone to seasickness, and anyone whose real goal is the islands themselves.
- **A tour from Panama City** is wrong for travellers who need luxury comfort on the islands, or who cannot face a 5:00 AM start and a mountain road.
- **A private charter** is wrong for solo travellers on a budget, and for anyone who wants Guna community life to be the centre of the trip; a boat gives you anchorages, not villages.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Cartagena sailing trip a San Blas tour?

Not really. It is a passage between Colombia and Panama that spends part of its time in the archipelago. You will swim and snorkel in San Blas, but the stops are chosen by the sailing route rather than by which islands are best.

### Which option is cheapest?

Per person, a shared tour from Panama City is usually the most economical way to see San Blas itself; current rates are on our prices page. Sailing crossings are priced per berth by each boat, and private charters are quoted per vessel.

### Which option is best if I get seasick?

A tour from Panama City. The crossing from Cartí to the islands is short, while a sailing passage means days in open water. The winding road to Cartí is the leg to prepare for.

### Do I pay the Guna Yala entry tax on every option?

On tours and stays from Panama City, the comarca tax of $22 for foreigners and $7 for residents is paid in cash and is not included in the price. On a sailing crossing or a charter, ask the operator how the comarca's fees are handled.

### Can I charter a private boat for just one day?

Yes. The Super VIP private day tour gives your group a private 4x4 and boat for the day. Multi-day crewed charters are arranged separately, with dates and group size discussed in advance.
