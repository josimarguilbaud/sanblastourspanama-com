**San Blas, properly Guna Yala, is a self-governed Indigenous archipelago in eastern Panama with no towns, hotels or nightlife, reached by 4x4 and boat from Panama City. Bocas del Toro is a developed island province near Costa Rica, usually reached by a domestic flight, with hotels, bars, surf and roads. They are different trips, not rivals.**

## The comparison in one table

| | San Blas (Guna Yala) | Bocas del Toro |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Eastern Caribbean coast of Panama | Western Caribbean coast, next to Costa Rica |
| What it is | Autonomous Indigenous comarca | A regular Panamanian province |
| Population, 2023 census | 32,016 in the whole comarca | 159,228 in the province |
| Who runs tourism | The Guna, by their own law | An open market, local and foreign-owned |
| Getting there from Panama City | 4x4 to Cartí, then a boat | Domestic flight, or most of a day overland |
| Where you sleep | Simple Guna-run cabins | Hostels, hotels, resorts |
| Evenings | Dinner, stars, silence | Restaurants, bars, live music |
| Phone and wifi | No wifi, patchy signal | Normal in town |
| Cash machines | None in the territory | In Bocas Town |
| Scuba diving | Not allowed for visitors | Available |
| Sensible minimum | One day, ideally one night | Three or four days |

The population figures come from Panama's statistics institute, [INEC, 2023 census](https://www.inec.gob.pa/archivos/P0579518620231213140655Cuadro%2005.pdf). The Bocas number covers the whole province, mainland included, but the contrast still says something real: one place is a set of island communities, the other a province with towns.

## Two different kinds of place

The deepest difference is not the beaches, it is who is in charge. Guna Yala is a comarca, a territory the Guna govern through their own congresses under Panamanian law. Their [Fundamental Law](https://www.gunayala.org.pa/Ley%20Fundamenta.Gunayala..pdf) reserves all tourism to the Guna and declares the land inalienable, so no outsider can buy an island or build a hotel. That is why there is no resort strip, no beach club and no town to wander: the absence is a decision. Our page on [Guna Yala](/guna-yala) explains the territory in more depth.

Bocas del Toro works like most Caribbean destinations. Bocas Town, on Isla Colón, is a real town with streets, shops, docks full of water taxis and hotels at every price. The islands around it mix Afro-Caribbean heritage, Ngäbe communities and a large community of foreign residents and business owners. You can rent a bike, eat somewhere different every night and plan as you go.

## Getting there

San Blas is the closer of the two in hours. A 4x4 collects you in Panama City around 5:00 AM, drives about two and a half hours to the port of Cartí, and boats take you to the islands; the full chain is explained in our guide to [getting to San Blas](/guides/how-to-get-to-san-blas).

Bocas del Toro is on the far side of the country. Most visitors take a domestic flight of about an hour from Panama City's Albrook airport to Isla Colón. The overland alternative, a long bus ride to the coast near Almirante and then a water taxi, takes most of a day. Travellers coming from Costa Rica often cross the border by land and continue to the islands from there.

There is no direct transport between the two archipelagos. Combining them means routing through Panama City.

## Where you sleep, and what that says about the trip

In San Blas you sleep in cabins run by Guna families: thatched roofs, sand underfoot, meals cooked by your hosts, shared bathrooms in some types and no air conditioning. There are five cabin types, from a shared dorm to an overwater cabin, set out in our [overnight stays guide](/guides/overnight-stays). Comfort is simple on purpose.

In Bocas you choose your level, from party hostels to quiet eco-lodges and resorts. If air conditioning, a proper shower and a restaurant menu matter to you, that alone may decide it.

## What you do all day

San Blas days are about the water and the islands themselves: hopping between small cays, wading at the Piscina Natural, snorkelling over the Perro Chico wreck, visiting a Guna community and buying a mola from the woman who stitched it. Scuba diving is not part of it; tanks are not allowed for visitors, as our [answer on diving](/blog/can-you-scuba-dive-in-san-blas) explains.

Bocas offers more variety. Isla Bastimentos National Marine Park, created in 1988 as Panama's first marine national park, protects reefs, mangroves and the Cayos Zapatilla. There are surf breaks, dive shops, boat tours, jungle walks and cacao farms that take visitors. The beaches are lovely, but the water rarely has the open-sea clarity of the outer Guna cays.

## Culture: a living territory or a mixed Caribbean town

Visiting San Blas means entering someone's territory. You pay a comarca entry tax at the border, $22 for foreigners and $7 for residents, in cash or by card to the driver on our tours, and not included in tour prices. Communities may ask a small fee, photographs of people need permission, and much of the visible culture, from molas to the congress house, belongs to daily life rather than to a show.

Bocas has its own deep history, including Afro-Antillean communities descended from Caribbean migrants and the Ngäbe people, but you experience it as a guest in a busy town rather than as a visitor crossing into a self-governed land.

## Connectivity, cash and comfort

San Blas has no wifi on the islands and the phone signal comes and goes. There are no cash machines and no card terminals, so you bring all the cash you need from the city; our post on [cash in San Blas](/blog/do-you-need-cash-in-san-blas) explains how much. Bocas Town has cash machines, card payments in many places and normal connectivity.

## So which one fits you?

- **Choose San Blas** if you want the clearest water and emptiest sand, a culture you will not see anywhere else, and a trip that fits into one or two days from Panama City. If San Andrés, Roatán or Los Roques are on your shortlist too, [see how San Blas compares with them](/blog/san-blas-vs-san-andres-roatan-los-roques).
- **Choose Bocas del Toro** if you want a base for several days, nightlife, surf, diving, comfortable hotels and the freedom to improvise.
- **Do both** if you have a week or more. A practical order: fly to Bocas for three or four days, return to Panama City, then spend a day or a night in San Blas before you fly home.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which has better beaches, San Blas or Bocas del Toro?

For clear water and small, empty white-sand cays, San Blas. Bocas has beautiful beaches, especially inside its marine park, but more boats, more development and usually less open-sea clarity.

### Can I visit both on one trip?

Yes, with at least a week. There is no direct link between them, so plan Bocas as a flight from Panama City and San Blas as a separate trip from the city, one before or after the other.

### Is there nightlife in San Blas?

No. There are no bars, clubs or towns built for visitors. Evenings on an overnight stay mean dinner with your hosts, the sky and early sleep. For nightlife, Bocas Town is the place.

### Which is better for families?

Both work, for different reasons. San Blas is easy for a short trip, with very calm, shallow water at places like the Piscina Natural. Bocas suits families who want hotel comfort, restaurants and several days in one base.

### How many days do I need for each?

San Blas can be seen in a day from Panama City, though a night on an island is far better. Bocas del Toro needs at least three days to justify the flight and to see more than the town.

### Can you scuba dive in either?

In Bocas del Toro, yes; there are dive shops in town. In Guna Yala, scuba diving is prohibited for visitors, and the islands are explored by snorkelling instead.
