We would rather talk you out of this one than sell it to you badly. Most San Blas islands have weak mobile signal or none at all, there is no wifi to speak of, and nothing here resembles a coworking space. If your job needs dependable video calls every day, book a holiday instead — you will enjoy the islands more and your colleagues will hear from you.

## Who it does work for

The people this suits are specific. Someone already travelling through Panama for weeks who needs a few hours of writing or admin every couple of days. A freelancer between projects who wants the inbox checked, not answered live. Someone whose deadlines are weekly rather than hourly. What they have in common is that their work is asynchronous and forgiving, and that they are honest with themselves about the difference between working remotely and simply being away.

## What is genuinely realistic

Islands nearer the mainland tend to have marginally better coverage than the outer cays, and marginally better still means intermittent. The workable pattern is a morning attempt and no promises: whatever the signal allows early on, then the rest of the day off. Power matters as much as signal — electricity is limited and does not reach every cabin, so a laptop and a power bank are a pair, not a choice.

## The two days you will not work at all

Count them before you plan. The journey is a pre-dawn pickup, a couple of hours of mountain road and a boat crossing at each end, and the return lands you back in the city in the afternoon. Whatever you tell your clients, the arrival and departure days are travel days, and a maximum of three nights means the whole trip contains fewer usable working hours than the photos suggest.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there wifi?
No, not in any sense you could plan around. There is no island network to log into and no coworking infrastructure anywhere in the archipelago. Anything that gets through does so over intermittent mobile coverage, and on many islands not even that.

### Will my roaming plan or eSIM work?
It makes no difference where there is no coverage to roam onto. A local plan may help slightly on islands closer to the mainland, but the limiting factor is the network reaching the island at all, not which company you pay.

### Can I take a video call?
Treat it as a no. Some travellers get one through and describe it as luck rather than a service. If a specific call cannot be missed, either move it before you leave or move the trip — this is the single most common way a workation here goes wrong.

### How will I charge a laptop?
Bring a power bank and charge whenever the opportunity appears. Electricity on most islands is limited and is not distributed to every cabin, so a full battery is something you protect rather than something you assume.

### Should I bring the laptop at all?
Think about it honestly. It travels in an open boat with spray on the crossing, then lives in sand and salt air for several days, on islands with no shop and no repair anywhere nearer than Panama City. Plenty of guests bring a notebook and a phone instead, and get more done.
