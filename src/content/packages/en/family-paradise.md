Parents booking San Blas on their own almost always pick the tour that visits the most islands, on the reasonable theory that more is better value. With children it's the opposite. Every extra stop is another stretch of open water, and open water — not the islands — is what empties a small child's patience and fills a parent's afternoon with worry.

## What actually wears a child out here

Three things, in this order: the boat, the sun, and the early start. Nothing on the island itself is difficult. So the whole shape of this concept is subtraction — shorter crossings by favouring calmer, shallower islands near the embarkation point, a private family cabin rather than a shared dorm, and a schedule loose enough that a slow morning doesn't wreck the day. Sand, shallow water and a hammock keep children occupied for far longer than any planned activity does.

## The five a.m. problem

Pickup in Panama City is before dawn, and that is the single hardest part of the trip with young children. Two nights is a much kinder format than one, because the arrival day is written off as a travel day and the middle day starts on the island instead of in a vehicle. Pack for the car as carefully as for the beach: something to sleep against, motion sickness medicine given before you set off rather than once someone feels ill, and snacks for the road.

## What to tell us first

Ages, how each child handles a small boat, and any allergy or food refusal that would ruin a meal. Those three lines shape the plan more than your dates do.

## Frequently asked questions

### What age does this start to make sense?
There is no minimum age, but there is a comfort threshold, and it has more to do with the two-and-a-half-hour mountain road than with the islands. Families with babies do come. Families with a child who is miserable in a car seat often have a better trip a year later.

### Are there life jackets for children?
Yes, and they are mandatory — for children and adults alike, at all times on the boats, including for confident swimmers. If your child has never worn one, it's worth trying one on before the trip so the first time isn't at a pier at seven in the morning.

### What will the children eat?
Meals are cooked by the Guna host family: fresh fish, rice, plantain, fruit. It's simple food rather than a children's menu, so tell us in advance about allergies or a very narrow eater, and bring backup snacks. There are no shops on the islands and no pharmacy, so anything you might need for a sick stomach travels with you.

### Is there a separate rate for children?
Yes, children have their own rate, and it varies by cabin type and by tour — one of the day tours has no child discount at all. Ask when you write; the booking tool always has the current figure.

### Can the grandparents come too?
Sometimes, and it's worth being honest about it. The trip is not wheelchair accessible, involves boarding boats from the water's edge and walking on sand, and operators advise against it for anyone with back or hip problems or a condition made worse by sun and boat travel. Tell us what the limitation is and we'll say plainly whether it works.
