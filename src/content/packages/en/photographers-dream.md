Photographers who come back from San Blas disappointed almost always made the same booking mistake: they took a day trip. A day trip is designed for coverage, and its hours are the hours the light is worst — you arrive mid-morning and leave mid-afternoon, which is precisely the window a landscape photographer would sit out.

## The two windows a day trip cannot reach

First light and last light both fall outside the schedule of any tour that returns to the mainland the same evening. Staying the night is the only way to be standing on sand when either one happens, and it also buys the third thing photographers ask about here: a genuinely dark sky. Guna Yala has essentially no light pollution, so the night sky is not a compromise version of one.

The trade this concept makes is explicit. You will visit fewer islands than a coverage itinerary would give you, and you will be somewhere specific when the light is good instead of somewhere average all day. Which island depends entirely on what you shoot — open water and reef reward a remote cluster with no other boats in frame; portraits and village life ask for somewhere inhabited; a foreground of palms and a jetty is a different island again. Tell us the subject, not just the dates.

## What boats and salt do to equipment

Everything here arrives by open boat, which means spray on the crossing and sand everywhere afterwards. Hard cases are difficult: space on the boats is limited and large luggage is not carried. A dry bag that closes properly does more for your gear than any amount of care once you're wet, and lens cloths disappear faster than you think.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I photograph the Guna people?
Ask first, every time. Homes, canoes and daily life are not a backdrop, and some areas of inhabited islands are residential rather than open to visitors. A friendly request usually gets a yes, and buying a mola directly from the woman who stitched it does more for the relationship than any explanation. Your guide can help you ask.

### Can I fly a drone?
Don't assume it. Guna Yala is an autonomous territory and permission is a matter for the community whose island you are on, not something a tour can grant on your behalf. Ask us before you plan a shot around it, and be prepared for the answer to be no.

### How do I keep batteries charged for several days?
Bring more capacity than you think you need, plus a power bank. Electricity on the islands is limited and does not reach every cabin, so treat charging as an occasional opportunity rather than a nightly routine.

### How bad is the sand and salt for gear?
Manageable if you plan for it, punishing if you don't. Changing lenses on a beach with any wind is how sensors get ruined, so change inside the cabin when you can, keep a sealed bag for the crossing, and rinse your hands before touching anything.

### Is the night sky really that dark?
Yes — there is no grid lighting across most of the archipelago, which is why one of the overnight options exists purely for sleeping under the open sky. Bring a tripod if long exposures are part of the plan; there is nothing to steady a camera against on a beach.
