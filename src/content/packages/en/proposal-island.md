Only one of you is reading this. That single fact shapes everything below, because a proposal is the one trip where the person planning it cannot talk it through with the person it's for, and has to get it right anyway.

## Why the evening is the whole trick

Almost everything that goes wrong with a proposal in San Blas is a scheduling problem, not a scenery problem. Day-trip boats arrive mid-morning and leave mid-afternoon, so the middle of the day is when the beaches have the most people on them and the least privacy. By early evening the visitors are gone and whoever stayed the night has the island more or less to themselves. That is the window, and it is the only real reason this concept is built around an overnight stay rather than a day out.

Everything else follows from it: an accommodation with its own private access, a stretch of light that doesn't have to be shared, and no boat waiting to leave in twenty minutes.

## Keeping it secret while you book it

Tell us in the first message that it's a surprise. We'll keep it out of anything the other person might read over your shoulder, and we'll talk logistics with you rather than with both of you. This matters more than people expect: a booking conversation is exactly where a surprise leaks.

## What we won't pretend to be

There is no in-house photographer, no prepared set of props, no rehearsed script. What there is, is real: a genuinely quiet island at the right hour, a guide who knows the place, and an operator willing to tell you plainly which of your ideas will work here and which one belongs in a different country. If you have something specific in mind, describe it and we'll answer honestly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I keep it a surprise if we're booking the trip together?
Write from your own phone, say up front that it's a proposal, and tell us exactly what your partner can and cannot be told. From that point we treat it as two conversations: one about a nice trip, and one about the plan. Decide early who is supposedly organising the holiday, because that's the story that has to hold.

### Where do I keep the ring?
On you, not in a bag. The trip involves an open boat, spray on the crossing, luggage being handed between hands, and sand in everything afterwards. A small hard case in a pocket you don't open for anything else is the version that survives.

### What if the weather ruins the evening?
Rain here usually arrives as a short heavy burst and clears, so a lost sunset is more often delayed than cancelled. If the date is emotionally fixed and you can't bear the risk, add the second night: it turns one chance at the light into two.

### Should I tell the guide?
Yes, and tell us in advance too. A guide who knows what's happening can put you in the right place at the right time and quietly keep other people out of it. A guide who doesn't know is a guide who cheerfully suggests a group activity at the worst possible moment.

### Can someone photograph it?
Ask, and get an honest answer rather than a promise. There's no photographer on staff, and arranging one on a remote island is a real logistical question, not a checkbox. Some couples solve it with a phone on a timer and prefer the result.
