An anniversary is the one trip people most often plan by repeating an old one. The honeymoon worked, so the tenth year gets the honeymoon itinerary again — same route, same pace, ten years of different life in between. This concept starts somewhere else: what do you want to be different this time?

## The couples who book it

Usually two kinds. Couples who came to Panama years ago and are back for a round number, and couples who have never been to San Blas but are marking something and don't want it to feel like a bus tour. A third kind shows up more than you'd expect: parents leaving the children with grandparents for the first time in years, who need the trip to be restful rather than impressive.

## Why the extra night changes the mood

Two nights instead of one turns a trip into a stay. The first day is spent arriving — a pre-dawn pickup, a couple of hours of mountain road, then a boat — and nobody is at their most romantic at the end of it. With two nights, the arriving day is just the arriving day, and the middle day belongs entirely to you: no pickup time, no boat to catch, nothing scheduled. That middle day is the whole point of the format.

## The detail people forget to mention

Tell us it's an anniversary. It sounds obvious, and it's the single most commonly skipped line in the first message. Knowing which night is the night lets the host family plan dinner around it and lets us aim the quiet stretch of the stay at the day that matters, instead of spreading it evenly across three.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can something be arranged for the dinner that night?
Within reason, yes. The kitchen is a Guna host family, not a hotel restaurant, so think a dinner served apart from the group at the hour you choose rather than a plated tasting menu. Ask early — the further ahead you tell us, the more they can plan for.

### How is this different from the one-night romantic escape?
Length and intent. The one-night version is an overwater night for a couple who wants the view and the privacy. This one adds a full unscheduled day and is built around marking an occasion, which usually means a slower pace and a bit more input from you about what the days contain.

### How far ahead should we book?
Any overnight stay needs at least three days' notice to confirm a cabin, but for a fixed anniversary date, weeks is more realistic. Cabins are limited and cannot be added; if the date is immovable, treat it as the first thing to lock in.

### Can we go back to the same island as last time?
Ask, and we'll tell you honestly. Stays are confirmed by cabin type rather than by island, and the specific island depends on availability near your date — so if a particular one matters to you, name it early rather than assume it.

### What if it rains on our free day?
Most rain here arrives as a short heavy burst and then clears, so a free day rarely gets written off. Nothing is scheduled to miss anyway, which is the quiet advantage of the format: a rainy hour on a covered deck costs you nothing you'd booked.
